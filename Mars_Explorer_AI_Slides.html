Operational Context

The Challenge: The Lethal Martian Frontier

1. Critical Water Scarcity

  • Human survival requires native In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU).
  • Water produces breathable oxygen (2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂) and return rocket fuel (CH₄).
  • Hauling water from Earth exceeds $100k/kg; finding shallow buried ice is life-or-death.

2. Extreme Terrain & Cold

  • Steep crater scarps (>20°) cause fatal astronaut slips and suit tears.
  • Soft dust ripples trap rovers; hidden basalt scree prevents traverse.
  • Temperatures plunge to -125°C, causing catastrophic suit battery drain.

3. Radiation & Return Limits

  • Mars lacks a global dipole magnetic field; surface GCR exposure reaches 0.65 mGy/day.
  • Astronauts have limited walking endurance and suit consumables.
  • Crews require a guaranteed 15% life-support reserve margin before returning.

The Solution: Mars Explorer AI

Autonomous Mission Intelligence

Mars Explorer AI transforms multi-mission planetary remote sensing data into an interactive, high-precision surface map. It equips astronauts and mission controllers to plan safe excursions and pinpoint water resources.

  • 8 Scientific Map Layers: Stackable and fader-controlled remote sensing.

  • Multi-Criteria A* Pathfinding: Shortest, Safest, Science, and Ice routes.
  • Real-time EVA Simulator: Minute-by-minute consumables telemetry.
  • Exportable Mission Plans: JSON for flight computers, Markdown briefs for crew.

Landing Sites

Three Diverse Martian Environments

Jezero Crater

18.44° N, 77.45° E · Ancient Lakebed & Delta

Bound Water in Clays

  • River delta explored by NASA Perseverance rover.
  • No shallow ice at equator; water locked in hydrated smectite clays (~3–6% wt) extractable via heating.
  • Prime target for astrobiological biosignature research.

Arcadia Planitia

46.20° N, 192.40° E · Shallow Ice Plains

Sheet Ice 0.3–2 m Deep

  • Smooth northern volcanic plains identified by SWIM project.
  • Massive sheet ice just beneath surface dust; easily accessible with hand drills.
  • Ideal human landing zone for drinking water and propellant ISRU.

Korolev Crater

73.30° N, 164.60° E · Frozen Ice Lake

1.8 km-Thick Pure Ice Dome

  • 82 km impact crater preserving ~2,200 km³ of pristine water ice.
  • Permanent cold-trap preserves clean surface ice year-round.
  • Extreme cold (-110°C) and slick ice require specialized spike boots.
Route Optimization

Multi-Criteria A* Pathfinding Engine

🟢 Safest Route (Human-Rated)

  • Strictly restricts slopes to <14°, penalizing dangerous 20°+ scarps.
  • Contours around loose sand dune fields to eliminate slipping and suit tears.
  • Maintains steady metabolic consumption and energy expenditure.

⚪ Shortest Route (Direct Traverse)

  • Direct Euclidean distance minimization between Habitat and Target.
  • Highlights the high hazard penalty of cutting across 25°+ cliffs.
  • Demonstrates the vital difference between distance and survivability.

🟣 Science Route (Sample Return)

  • Dynamically detours to intercept high-value geological waypoints.
  • Visits Belva crater rim, sedimentary mudstones, and olivine outcrops.
  • Maximizes sample diversity and mission scientific return per km.

🔵 Ice / Water Route (ISRU Survey)

  • Weights the graph toward verified shallow ice and exposed ice scarps.
  • Maximizes time along extractable water-ice corridors.
  • Identifies prime hand-drill sites yielding >700 L water/m³.

Life-Support Telemetry Tracking

  • Radiation Accumulator: Tracks cumulative GCR dose using MSL RAD data across open plateaus vs. shielded depressions.

Mission Intelligence

MISA: 5 Core Innovations & AI Decision Support

1. Real-Time NASA Data Explorer

Interactive point inspection providing NASA-calibrated elevation (MOLA), temperature (THEMIS), mineralogy (CRISM), and ice radar (SWIM/SHARAD) with dataset reliability scores.

2. AI Danger & Risk Prediction

Explains WHY an area is hazardous: synergetic modeling combining 20°+ slopes, boulder distributions, extreme cold (-85°C), and radiation flux into actionable crew advisories.

3. Smart Water Extraction Planner

Calculates overburden ice depth, required drilling equipment (hand auger vs rotary), energy cost (~kWh/m³), and yields of potable water, breathing O₂, and rocket CH₄.

4. MISA & Emergency Return Abort

Mars Intelligent Survival Assistant monitors suit O₂ and battery in real time, enforcing a strict 15% life-support reserve and triggering emergency return paths when margins drop.
